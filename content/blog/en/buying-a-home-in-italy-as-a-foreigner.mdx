A foreigner can buy property in Italy by getting a codice fiscale, agreeing the price in an offer or preliminary contract, and signing the deed (*rogito*) before an Italian notary. The main cost on top of the price is registration tax: 9% of the taxable value from a private seller, or 2% if you qualify for the first-home benefits, which most non-resident buyers will not.

## Can foreigners buy property in Italy?

According to the Italian Notariat (Consiglio Nazionale del Notariato), EU citizens face no special conditions when buying real estate. For non-EU citizens the notary verifies your status before the deed, so check with a notary early whether a permit or reciprocity condition applies to your nationality. We do not list country-by-country rules here because we have no official table to cite.

## Step by step

1. **Get a codice fiscale.** The Italian tax code is needed for real estate transactions, and a non-resident can delegate a representative to request it at the Agenzia delle Entrate. Our guide on the [codice fiscale for foreigners](/blog/how-to-get-a-codice-fiscale-to-buy-in-italy) covers the documents and who can apply for you.
2. **Choose your own notary early.** The Notariat recommends contacting a notary from the start of the negotiations, before you sign an offer or preliminary contract. The choice of notary is the buyer's: it should not be imposed by the agent or the bank.
3. **Make an offer and sign the preliminary contract.** The Notariat explains that an offer accepted in writing binds the seller, and that the accepted proposal converts into a preliminary contract obliging both sides to sign the final deed. It recommends having this contract drawn up by the notary as an authenticated or public deed, so that it can be transcribed in the land registry and the property is reserved for you.
4. **Let the notary check the property.** Before the deed, the notary identifies the owners, and checks them against the property registers. The Agenzia delle Entrate adds that the deed must cite the cadastral data and filed floor plans or it is void, and that the parties must declare the property matches those records, unless a technician certifies it.
5. **Sign the deed.** The deed is read out to the parties. According to the Notariat, if a party does not speak Italian the notary can have it translated, or an interpreter chosen by the parties can attend. Payment details, including any agent and the fees paid, must be declared in the deed. The Agenzia delle Entrate warns that omitted or false information can bring a fine of €500 to €10,000 and criminal liability.
6. **Registration.** The notary pays registration, mortgage and cadastral taxes when the deed is registered, per the Agenzia delle Entrate.

If you cannot be in Italy on the day, ask your notary about signing through a representative with a power of attorney. We do not describe the formalities because we have not verified them in an official source.

## Taxes on the purchase

All figures below come from the Agenzia delle Entrate page on taxes when buying a home, as read on 2026-11-08.

| Seller | Registration tax | Mortgage tax | Cadastral tax | VAT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private individual | 9% (minimum €1,000) | €50 | €50 | None |
| Company, VAT-exempt sale (general rule) | 9% (minimum €1,000) | €50 | €50 | None |
| Company charging VAT (for example a builder selling a recently completed home) | €200 | €200 | €200 | 10%, or 22% for categories A/1, A/8, A/9 |

The registration tax due can be lower than the table suggests when tax was already paid on a deposit under a preliminary contract, because that amount is deducted.

### The price-value option

If you are a private individual buying a residential property and not acting for a business, you can ask the notary in the deed to calculate taxes on the cadastral value instead of the price. For a non-first-home purchase the base is cadastral income × 1.05 × 120. The agency's own example: cadastral income of €900 and a price of €200,000 give a base of €113,400, so registration tax is €10,206 instead of €18,000. The request must be in the deed itself, and the real price must be stated. According to the agency, notary fees fall by 30%, but concealing part of the price brings recalculation and a 50–100% penalty on the difference.

## First-home benefits: do they apply to you?

The first-home (*prima casa*) rate is 2% registration tax (minimum €1,000) with €50 mortgage and €50 cadastral tax when buying from a private seller. When the seller charges VAT, VAT is 4% and the other taxes are €200 each.

The Agenzia delle Entrate lists conditions that matter for foreigners:

- the home must be in a municipality where you reside, or where you work or carry out an activity (study and volunteering count);
- if you live in another municipality, you must move your residence there within **18 months** and declare that intention in the deed;
- the home must not be a luxury category (A/1, A/8, A/9);
- you cannot sell or donate it within **5 years**, with an exception for buying another home within a year to use as a main residence;
- if you already own a home bought with the benefits, you must sell it within **2 years** for deeds from 1 January 2025.

Losing the benefits means the higher taxes, interest and, in some cases, a 30% penalty. A buyer who plans a holiday home or investment property should plan on the 9% rate and ask the notary to confirm.

## Costs this guide does not quote

Notary fees, agent commissions, translation costs and any mortgage costs are separate. We have not found an official figure for them, so we leave them out: ask your notary for a written estimate of all costs, including taxes, before you sign the preliminary contract.

## After you buy

- Keep the registered deed and your codice fiscale certificate.
- If you claimed the first-home benefits, diary the 18-month residence deadline and the 5-year holding period.
- If you plan to rent the home out, tax rules for rental income are not covered here: check the Agenzia delle Entrate or a tax adviser.

## Quick checklist

- Codice fiscale in hand, through a delegate if needed
- Notary chosen by you, contacted before any signed offer
- Seller type known (private, company with or without VAT)
- First-home conditions checked honestly, including the 18-month residence rule
- Price-value option discussed with the notary if you buy from a private seller
- Written estimate of total costs before signing
