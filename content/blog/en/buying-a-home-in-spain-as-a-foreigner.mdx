Spain is one of the easiest countries in Europe for foreigners to buy a home: there is no special permit to ask for and the process is the same for Spaniards and non-Spaniards. What catches people out is the paperwork order and the costs on top of the price. This guide walks through both.

## 1. Get your NIE first

The **NIE** (*Número de Identidad de Extranjero*) is the tax and identity number Spain gives to foreigners. You need it to sign at the notary, pay the taxes, register the property and open most bank accounts.

- You can request it **in Spain** (police foreigners' offices, by appointment) or **at a Spanish consulate** in your country.
- In Spain it is usually resolved within days; through a consulate it can take a few weeks, so start early.
- There is a small administrative fee (form 790, code 012).

A lawyer or *gestor* can apply on your behalf with a power of attorney, which is useful if you can't travel.

## 2. Open a Spanish bank account

It is not strictly required by law, but in practice you will need one: to pay the deposit and the taxes, and later to have utility bills and the local property tax (IBI) paid by direct debit.

## 3. Check the property before you pay anything

Ask for, or order yourself, these documents:

- **Nota simple** from the land registry (*Registro de la Propiedad*): confirms who the legal owner is and whether there are mortgages, liens or other charges on the property.
- **Cadastral data** (*Catastro*): checks that the surface area and use match what you are told.
- **Energy performance certificate**, required to sell or rent.
- Proof that **community fees** and **IBI** are paid up to date.
- For older or rural homes: that extensions and outbuildings are legal and registered.

Many buyers hire an independent lawyer (not one recommended by the seller) for these checks; fees are often around 1% of the price.

## 4. Reservation and deposit contract

Once you agree on a price, it is common to sign a **private deposit contract** (*contrato de arras*), often with around 10% of the price. With the most usual type (*arras penitenciales*), if you back out you lose the deposit, and if the seller backs out they return it doubled. Read which type you sign.

## 5. Sign at the notary

The sale is completed by signing the **deed** (*escritura pública*) before a Spanish notary, who checks identities and the registry status and explains the deed. You pay the rest of the price at signing, usually by bank transfer or banker's draft. After signing, the deed is filed with the land registry and you become the registered owner.

## 6. Taxes and fees on top of the price

The biggest item depends on whether the home is new or second-hand:

| Cost | Resale home | New build (first sale by a developer) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main tax | Transfer tax (ITP), set by each region: from about 6% (e.g. Madrid) to 10% or more (e.g. Catalonia, with higher brackets for expensive homes) | VAT of 10% on homes, plus stamp duty (AJD), usually around 0.5–1.5% depending on the region |
| Notary | Regulated fees, usually in the hundreds of euros up to a few thousand | Same |
| Land registry | Regulated fees, usually in the hundreds of euros | Same |
| Lawyer / gestor | Often around 1% (optional, but recommended) | Same |

Two things to know about the transfer tax: it is calculated on the **price or the official reference value** (*valor de referencia* of the Catastro), whichever is higher, and several regions have reduced rates for young buyers, large families or primary homes. A common budget for a resale is **8–13% on top of the price**.

The municipal capital gains tax (*plusvalía municipal*) is normally paid by the seller.

## 7. Mortgages for non-residents

Spanish banks lend to non-residents, but usually finance a lower share of the price than for residents and ask for proof of income in your home country. Get a pre-approval before signing the deposit contract.

## 8. After you buy

- **Local property tax (IBI)** every year, set by the town hall.
- **Community fees** if the home is in a building or development.
- **Non-resident income tax**: if you are not tax resident in Spain, you declare a deemed income for a home you don't rent (form 210), or the actual rent if you do.
- **No residency through property anymore**: the investor (golden) visa for buying real estate was abolished with effect from 3 April 2025. Buying a home does not give you a right to live in Spain; non-EU citizens need another type of visa.

## Quick checklist

1. NIE requested (in Spain or at a consulate)
2. Spanish bank account open
3. Financing pre-approved, if you need it
4. Independent lawyer checking nota simple, Catastro and debts
5. Deposit contract signed, knowing which type of *arras* it is
6. Budget of 8–13% (resale) or 10% VAT + AJD (new build) on top of the price
7. Deed signed at the notary and filed with the land registry
