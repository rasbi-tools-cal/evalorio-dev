Foreigners can buy property in Portugal, and the first practical step is a Portuguese tax number (NIF). After that the process runs through a checked set of property documents, a deed signed before a notary, and registration at the land registry. On top of the price you pay IMT (transfer tax) and stamp duty, and each year IMI (property tax). This guide follows the order used in the government's own purchase guide.

## 1. Get your NIF

The **NIF** (*Número de Identificação Fiscal*) is the tax number you need for the purchase, and it is also what banks and utilities ask for. According to gov.pt:

- The application is **free**.
- If you live in Portugal, you apply in person at a tax office (*Serviço de Finanças*), by appointment.
- If you are a **non-resident**, you provide your passport or national ID and a document showing your address abroad. Non-residents without a Portuguese address may need a **tax representative** established in Portugal, and some applicants can submit the request online through the Portal das Finanças.

The exact rules depend on where you live (inside or outside the EU/EEA) and change from time to time, so check the current guidance on gov.pt and the Portal das Finanças before you apply.

## 2. Check the property documents

The government's guide lists the documents to have in order before the deed:

- Civil and tax identification of the parties.
- The **land registry certificate** (*certidão de teor*), showing the owner and any charges.
- The **energy certificate**.
- A **building licence**, if the building was built after August 1951.
- The technical housing file, where applicable.

A Portuguese lawyer or *solicitador* can check this for you. The guide also lists insurance among the steps (fire, flood and theft cover are named). If you take a mortgage, ask your lender which cover it requires.

## 3. Promissory contract (optional)

Before the deed, buyer and seller commonly sign a **promissory contract of purchase and sale** (*contrato-promessa*), which sets out rights and duties of both sides. gov.pt calls it optional. Read the clauses on the deposit and on what happens if either side withdraws.

## 4. Sign the deed

The **deed** (*escritura*) is executed at a notary office, at a court registry, or through the **Casa Pronta** service. You must show proof that the transfer tax and stamp duty have been paid.

## 5. Register the purchase

Finally, you register the property in your name at the **Conservatória do Registo Predial** (land registry). Registration fees apply.

## Taxes and costs when you buy

| Item | What gov.pt and the tax authority say |
| --- | --- |
| IMT (transfer tax) | Calculated on the property value. For urban homes on the mainland, the rate is progressive by value bracket, starting at 0% for lower values and rising for higher ones. The bracket limits and deductions are updated, so check the current table on the Portal das Finanças |
| Stamp duty (*Imposto do Selo*) | 0.8% on the higher of the purchase price or the tax value (*valor patrimonial tributário*) |
| Stamp duty on a mortgage | 0.6% of the credit if it is over €5,000, according to the same guide |
| Registration | Land registry fees apply |
| IMI (annual property tax) | Charged every year on the assessed value of the property |

**Buyers aged 35 or under** may be exempt from IMT and stamp duty on a first home for permanent residence, under conditions on age, value and not owning other housing. The Portal das Finanças has a dedicated IMT Jovem page. Do not assume you qualify without checking it.

This guide does not give a total percentage for costs, because IMT depends on the value of the property and where it is. Ask your lawyer to calculate it for your purchase.

## After you buy

- **IMI** is billed every year by the tax authority.
- Keep proof of the deed and registration, and update your tax address if you move.
- If you will rent the home out, income tax rules for non-residents apply. Confirm them with the Portal das Finanças or an adviser.

## Quick checklist

1. NIF obtained (and a tax representative, if one is required in your case)
2. Land registry certificate, energy certificate and licence checked
3. Mortgage and insurance arranged, if needed
4. Promissory contract read carefully, deposit terms understood
5. IMT and stamp duty calculated and paid before the deed
6. Deed signed and purchase registered at the land registry
