You can sell a home in Spain without an estate agent: the law does not require one, and the sale is completed before a notary either way. What you take on yourself is the agent's work: the paperwork, the advertisement, the viewings and the negotiation. This guide follows the order of a private sale and points out the obligations that stay with the seller, including the tax points that matter most if you live abroad.

## 1. Get the energy certificate before you advertise

This is the step private sellers most often forget. According to the Ministry for the Ecological Transition, in the sale of an existing building the seller must give the buyer a copy of the **registered energy performance certificate** and attach the **energy label** to the sales contract. The ministry also says you must hold the certificate, signed by a competent technician, before you advertise the property.

Advertisements for a sale or rental, in any medium, must include the energy rating. The ministry lists some allowances: the label can be enlarged or reduced if its format and proportions stay legible, press ads may show only the letters for consumption and emissions, and an outdoor sign that shows only a phone number does not need the rating. The rules have been amended since 2021, so check the current text on the ministry page when you list.

## 2. Collect the documents the notary will ask for

The notaries' guidance lists what is checked before signing:

- **Ownership and charges.** The notary requests a note from the land registry showing the owner and any mortgage, seizure or other lien. If you still have a mortgage, plan how it will be cancelled at closing.
- **Cadastral reference.** The notary takes it from the latest **IBI** (local property tax) receipt, which the seller presents.
- **Community fees.** The notary checks that you are up to date. The buyer can ask for a certificate from the community secretary, with the president's approval, showing you owe nothing; the buyer may waive it after checking the fee status. If fees are owed, the notaries warn that the buyer may end up having to pay them.
- **Power of attorney, if someone signs for you.** The notaries say it must be an authorised copy signed by a notary, not a photocopy.

A folder with the title deed, the latest IBI receipt, the energy certificate and the community certificate makes your listing more credible and shortens the time between offer and signature.

## 3. Advertise and show the home

Give exact figures: surface area, number of rooms, community fees, annual IBI and what is included in the price. Show the energy label, as required. You can [publish your listing for free on Evalorio](/post): owners list without commission and buyers contact them directly. Buyers already browse [homes for sale in Spain](/spain/homes-for-sale) and city pages such as [apartments for sale in Madrid](/spain/madrid/apartments-for-sale).

We do not give a recommended asking price here. Evalorio's data has too few active listings per city and category to calculate a reliable median, and a price needs comparables from your own street. A registered valuer, a notary or recent sales in your building are better guides.

## 4. Sign a deposit contract with care

Once you and a buyer agree, it is common to sign a private contract with a deposit. Spain's Civil Code (article 1454) regulates the kind of deposit called *arras penitenciales*: the buyer who backs out loses it and the seller who backs out returns it doubled. Spanish case law quoted in BOE resolutions treats this kind of deposit as exceptional: the right to withdraw must clearly follow from the contract and is not presumed, and a payment called a "señal" may otherwise be taken as an advance on the price.

For you as seller this means: write down what the deposit is, who can withdraw and on what terms, and the date for the deed. Have the text checked by a lawyer or the notary. A generic template from the internet is the most common source of disputes.

## 5. Sign the deed at the notary

The notariado says a public deed is not compulsory for a sale between the parties, but recommends it. A private contract does not give the same legal security, and the deed is what you need to register the new owner in the land registry; only the deed has executive effect, and signing it equals delivery of the property. In practice, a buyer who pays with a mortgage or wants to be registered as owner will insist on it.

At signing, the notary checks identities and the registry status. If you still have a mortgage, agree with the notary and your bank beforehand how it will be settled and cancelled, and how the price will be paid. We have not verified those mechanics from an official source, so ask the notary to confirm the sequence.

## 6. Taxes for the seller

Selling is not free of tax, and the answer depends on your residency. We give the main points and the official sources; the amounts depend on your own purchase price and situation.

### Plusvalía municipal

This municipal tax on the increase in value of urban land is regulated in the law on local finances (articles 104 and 106). In a sale for consideration, the taxpayer is the seller. The law says the tax does not apply where it is shown that the land did not increase in value, and for that you must declare the transfer and provide the title documents of the sale and of your own purchase. If the seller is a non-resident individual, the buyer is the substitute taxpayer. Rates and procedures are set by each town hall, so ask yours.

### Capital gains tax

The Tax Agency's manual for non-residents explains that the gain is the transmission value minus the acquisition value. The acquisition value includes the price you paid, improvements, and acquisition costs and taxes; the transmission value is the sale price minus your own selling costs and taxes. For residents the gain is declared in the income tax return; non-residents file form 210. We do not state rates here because they depend on your residency and the tax year, and you should confirm them on the Tax Agency site or with an adviser.

### Reinvesting in a new main home

The Tax Agency says that if you sell your main home and reinvest in another main home within **two years** (counted from date to date, before or after the sale), the gain can be excluded from tax. The exemption is not automatic: you must state that you want it. If you reinvest only part of the proceeds, only the matching proportion of the gain is exempt. For non-residents, the Tax Agency extends this to residents of EU member states, Iceland, Norway and Liechtenstein.

### The 3% withholding if you live abroad

If you are a non-resident, the buyer must withhold **3% of the agreed price** and pay it to the Tax Agency with **form 211**, as a payment on account of your tax. According to the Agency, you then deduct it from the tax on your gain, and it applies even if the gain is exempt through reinvestment. The Tax Agency adds that if the amount withheld exceeds your final tax, you can obtain a refund of the excess. Ask your notary or adviser to agree who files form 211 before signing.

## 7. After the sale

- Make sure the registry shows the new owner and that your mortgage is cancelled.
- Cancel or transfer utilities.
- If you are a non-resident, file form 210 for the gain.
- Keep the deed, the invoices for improvements and the proof of your original purchase price for tax purposes.

## When an agent can still make sense

Selling alone takes time for calls, viewings and negotiation. If you live abroad, cannot attend viewings, or the property has complications such as inheritance, co-ownership or planning irregularities, a professional may be worth the cost. The legal steps above are the same either way.

## Quick checklist

1. Order the energy performance certificate and keep the registered copy.
2. Gather the title deed, the latest IBI receipt and the community certificate.
3. Advertise with the energy label shown.
4. Have the deposit contract checked before signing: what it is, who can withdraw, and the date for the deed.
5. Sign the public deed at the notary and cancel any mortgage.
6. Ask your town hall about plusvalía, and confirm the capital gains tax and any 3% withholding on the Tax Agency site.
7. Consider the two-year reinvestment rule if you are buying another main home.
