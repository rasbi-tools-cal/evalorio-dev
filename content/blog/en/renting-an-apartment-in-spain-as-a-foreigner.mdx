In Spain, renting a home as your main residence is governed by the Urban Leases Act (*Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos*, LAU), which applies to foreigners and Spaniards alike. The short version: the deposit is one month's rent, the contract runs for at least five years unless you leave earlier, and you cannot be charged agency fees. The details below come from the consolidated text published in the BOE, the official gazette.

## 1. Know which kind of contract you are signing

The act distinguishes between a **home let as a main residence** and other uses. Tourist lets are outside it: article 5 excludes the temporary letting of a home for tourist use, and sets 31 days as the maximum for that kind of letting. Temporary lets of a home are regulated separately, in article 9 bis. Ask the landlord which contract you are being offered and why. If you plan to live in the flat for a year or more, you want a standard main-residence lease.

## 2. Contract length and leaving early

- The length is freely agreed. If it is shorter than **five years** (seven if the landlord is a company), the contract is extended year by year until it reaches that minimum, unless you give 30 days' notice that you do not wish to renew.
- After the first year, the landlord is a private person and the contract states a need to use the home for themselves or a first-degree relative, the compulsory extension may not apply.
- After the minimum term, the contract is extended tacitly if nobody gives notice. The consolidated text on the BOE shows changes to article 10 (the length of these tacit extensions and the notice periods) taking effect on 15 November 2026, so check the version in force on the day you sign.

## 3. The deposit

Article 36 requires a cash deposit (*fianza*) of **one month's rent** for a home. During the first five years (seven for a company landlord) it is not updated. An extra guarantee, such as a bank guarantee, may be agreed, but it cannot exceed **two months' rent** for contracts of up to five years. A landlord cannot require you to take out rent-default insurance.

Article 17 adds that the landlord cannot demand more than one month's rent in advance.

## 4. Rent, payment and updates

- The rent is whatever the parties freely agree, except in areas the regional authorities have declared **tensioned residential markets**, where the act sets limits on the initial rent and increases. Check with the regional government whether the street you are looking at is in one.
- Unless agreed otherwise, rent is due monthly in the first seven days of the month.
- Payment must be made by electronic means. Cash is an exception, for a party who has no bank account or electronic access and asks for it.
- Rent can only be updated if the contract says so, on each anniversary, and the rise cannot exceed the change in the **IRAV** index named in article 18. The new rent is due from the month after you receive written notice.

## 5. Who pays what

| Item | What the act says |
| --- | --- |
| Agency or management fees, contract formalisation | Cannot be charged to the tenant of a home (art. 20) |
| Property taxes linked to the home (e.g. IBI) | Cannot be imposed on the tenant (art. 20) |
| General building costs (community fees) | Can be agreed to be paid by the tenant, in a building under horizontal property |
| Individual services (water, electricity) | Normally paid by the tenant under their own contracts |

Increases in agreed general costs, during the first five years, can only be agreed annually and cannot exceed twice the limit that applies to the rent.

## 6. Before you sign

1. Read the contract in a language you understand, or ask for a translation.
2. Check that the landlord is the owner or is authorised to let the property. A land registry extract (*nota simple*) shows who the owner is; see our [guide to buying in Spain](/blog/buying-a-home-in-spain-as-a-foreigner) for how it works.
3. Confirm the amount of the deposit and how it is returned.
4. Confirm whether rent updates are in the contract and which index applies.
5. Keep proof of every payment.
