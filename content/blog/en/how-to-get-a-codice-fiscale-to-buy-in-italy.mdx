You need a **codice fiscale** (Italian tax code) before you can buy property in Italy, and as a non-resident you can get it without moving: you delegate someone to request it for you at the Agenzia delle Entrate. Once you have it, the main cost on top of the price is registration tax, which depends on who sells and whether the first-home benefits apply.

## What the codice fiscale is for

The codice fiscale is an alphanumeric code issued by the Agenzia delle Entrate (the Italian Revenue Agency) to identify people who deal with Italian public bodies and companies. Italian consular pages list real estate transactions, inheritance, work, opening a bank account and university enrolment among the uses. For a purchase, it goes into the deed and the tax filings.

## How a non-resident gets it

1. **Delegate a representative.** The consular guidance says a foreign citizen who is not resident in Italy must delegate a representative to submit the request directly to an Agenzia delle Entrate office in Italy. The representative can be a trusted person or a professional, such as a lawyer.
2. **Prepare the documents.** Expect to provide a copy of your passport or ID, the request form, and a proof of the reason you need the code, such as the intended property purchase. Ask the Agenzia delle Entrate office or your representative for the current form.
3. **Collect the code.** Keep the certificate: the notary and your bank will ask for the number.

### When a consulate can help

Consulates only issue the code in limited cases. According to the Edinburgh consulate, they accept requests when the code is needed for online procedures, or when the person cannot delegate someone to apply through the Agenzia delle Entrate. The same page lists **property buyers** among the cases the consulate does *not* process: they use a delegate at the Agenzia delle Entrate. Procedures differ from one consulate to another, so check yours.

If you already live in Italy, different routes apply (for example, an AA4/8 form at the local Agenzia delle Entrate office for EU residents, per the same page).

## Taxes when you buy a home

The Agenzia delle Entrate publishes the taxes on a home purchase. They depend on the seller and on whether you qualify for the first-home (*prima casa*) benefits.

| Situation | Registration tax | Mortgage tax | Cadastral tax | VAT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private seller, no first-home benefits | 9% (minimum €1,000) | €50 | €50 | None |
| Company selling VAT-exempt, no first-home benefits | 9% (minimum €1,000) | €50 | €50 | None |
| Private seller, with first-home benefits | 2% (minimum €1,000) | €50 | €50 | None |
| Seller subject to VAT (typically new build) | €200 | €200 | €200 | 10%, or 22% for luxury categories A/1, A/8, A/9 |

The Agenzia delle Entrate also explains a **price-value** option for private purchases without first-home benefits: tax is calculated on the cadastral value instead of the declared price (cadastral rent × 1.05 × 120). It must be requested in the deed, the actual price must be stated, and the buyer must be a private individual buying a home. According to the agency, it limits the authorities' assessment powers and reduces notary fees by 30%.

The first-home benefits come with conditions set by the tax authority. Read its first-home page and ask the notary whether you qualify before assuming the 2% rate. Notary fees and agent commissions are separate costs; this guide does not quote them because we have no official figure to cite.

## Steps of the purchase

1. Get the codice fiscale (through a delegate, if you are abroad).
2. Agree the price and, if you use one, sign a preliminary contract.
3. Have a notary check the property and the seller's title.
4. Sign the deed (*rogito*) at the notary and pay the taxes above.
5. Keep the registered deed and update your tax records.

## Quick checklist

- Representative appointed and passport copy ready
- Codice fiscale certificate in hand
- Seller type known (private or company), so you know whether tax is 9% or VAT
- First-home eligibility checked with the notary
- Price-value option considered, if buying from a private seller
