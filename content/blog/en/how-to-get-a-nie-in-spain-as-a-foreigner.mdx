A NIE (*Número de Identidad de Extranjero*) is the identification number Spain gives to foreigners who have economic, professional or social ties to the country. You request it with form EX-15 and a fee paid on Form 790, either at the Spanish consulate for your place of residence or in Spain. It identifies you to the Spanish administration, but it does not give you the right to live in Spain.

This guide covers the application route, the documents and the fee mechanism as described by Spanish official pages. Requirements differ slightly between consulates, so treat the office that will handle your file as the final word.

## What a NIE is, and what it is not

According to the Spanish consulate in London, the NIE is for foreigners with economic, professional or social ties to Spain, and the reason is stated on the application form. The same page is explicit about its limits: the NIE "is not valid for residing" in Spain and does not prove residence. Anyone who wants to live in Spain should check whether a visa is needed first.

If you are buying or renting a home, the NIE is the number that many Spanish procedures ask for. Our guide to [buying a home in Spain as a foreigner](/blog/buying-a-home-in-spain-as-a-foreigner) shows where it fits in the purchase.

## Where to apply

You have two routes, both described on the consulate page:

1. **At the Spanish consular office for your place of residence**, usually by appointment.
2. **In Spain**, through the police authorities. The Policía Nacional e-office lists that requests are filed with the Dirección General de la Policía, through the Oficinas de Extranjería or a police station (Comisaría de Policía).

In both cases the consulate states that "physical presence of the applicant or of their representative is always required". A representative needs their own ID and a power of attorney that expressly authorises the NIE application. Notarised powers of attorney must be legalised or apostilled.

## Documents you need

The London consulate lists these for an adult applicant:

- **Form EX-15** (original and copy), completed and signed, stating the economic, professional or social reasons for the request.
- **Passport**: the original and a copy of the page with your photo and details. EU nationals may use their national ID card instead.
- **Proof of residence** within the consular district.
- **Form 790, code 012**, completed, with the fee paid.
- For a representative: their ID or passport and the power of attorney described above.
- For minors or people lacking legal capacity: a family book, birth certificate or guardianship document, plus a parent's or guardian's valid passport or ID.

Other consulates may ask for additional supporting papers, such as proof of the reason you give. Check the page of the office you will use before booking.

## The fee

The fee is paid with **Form 790, code 012**. The Policía Nacional page explains that you can generate the payment form online and pay at banks, savings banks and credit cooperatives, with no account needed, by presenting the payment document. That page does not state the amount and links to a separate fee table.

For one concrete figure, the London consulate lists **£8.65** for its application, paid by card at the consulate on the day of the appointment. This is the amount for that office only. Other offices may charge a different amount in a different currency.

## Appointment and timing

Consulates generally work by appointment. London asks applicants to request one by email, and says the NIE is usually issued in about two weeks on average, that force majeure can extend this, and that the consulate cannot speed up processing. The NIE is sent by email to the address on the application, so use one you check.

For the related **non-resident certificate** (*certificado de no residente*), the Policía Nacional page says the request must be resolved within five days of entering the competent office's register, and that silence in that period counts as a rejection. The certificate is valid for 3 months from issue. It is a different document from the NIE itself; use it only if a procedure asks for it.

## Quick checklist

1. Decide your route: consulate for your place of residence, or in Spain.
2. Book the appointment with the specific office.
3. Complete form EX-15 with your reason for the request.
4. Generate and pay Form 790, code 012, as the office instructs.
5. Bring your passport (or EU ID card), copies and proof of residence in the consular district.
6. Give an email address you check; the NIE may be sent electronically.
